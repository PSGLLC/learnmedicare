

MEDICARE AT 65: A PRACTICAL 2027 GUIDE
======================================

Coverage year: 2027 for the amounts below. Enrollment windows are
identified separately.
Publisher: Learn Medicare, an educational resource from Price
Services Group, LLC.

This guide is general education, not a coverage decision or
individualized insurance recommendation. Medicare eligibility and
enrollment depend on your circumstances. Confirm plan-specific
benefits and costs in the plan documents and Medicare Plan Finder.

[Download the printable 2027 guide
(PDF)](/docs/T65-Medicare-Guide.pdf). The yearless PDF address
serves the current edition; dated editions remain archived
separately.

START WITH THE FOUR PARTS
=========================

* Part A helps cover inpatient hospital care, skilled nursing
facility care after a qualifying inpatient stay, hospice, and some
home health care.
* Part B helps cover physician and outpatient services, preventive
care, and durable medical equipment.
* Part C (Medicare Advantage) is a private-plan way to receive Part
A and Part B benefits. Plans may use provider networks and may
include Part D. Benefits and cost sharing vary by plan and location.
* Part D is optional prescription drug coverage through private
plans, either standalone or included in some Medicare Advantage
plans.

Original Medicare does not have a general annual out-of-pocket
maximum for Part A and Part B services. Medicare Advantage plans
have plan-specific limits for covered Part A and Part B services;
read the plan's evidence of coverage. Drug coverage has separate
rules and limits. See Medicare's cost overview
(https://www.medicare.gov/basics/costs/medicare-costs).

WHEN CAN I ENROLL?
==================

INITIAL ENROLLMENT PERIOD (IEP)
===============================

For most people first eligible at 65, the IEP lasts seven months:
the three months before the month you turn 65, your birthday month,
and the three months after. The date coverage begins can depend on
when you enroll. If your birthday is on the first day of a month,
Medicare generally treats you as turning 65 in the prior month;
check Medicare's coverage-start table
(https://www.medicare.gov/basics/get-started-with-medicare/sign-up/when-does-medicare-coverage-start)
for your exact effective date.

ENROLLMENT THROUGH CURRENT EMPLOYMENT
=====================================

If you or your spouse has qualifying group health coverage based on
current employment, you may be able to delay Part B without a late
enrollment penalty. The Part B Special Enrollment Period generally
lasts eight months after the employment or group coverage ends,
whichever happens first. COBRA and retiree coverage do not extend
this Part B enrollment window. See Medicare's current-employment SEP
rules
(https://www.medicare.gov/basics/get-started-with-medicare/sign-up/when-does-medicare-coverage-start).
Ask the employer benefits administrator whether the coverage is
based on current employment and how it coordinates with Medicare. Do
not rely on employer size alone to decide which coverage pays first.

ANNUAL ENROLLMENT PERIOD (AEP)
==============================

The AEP runs October 15 through December 7 each year. Changes made
during this window generally take effect January 1 of the next year.
During the 2026 AEP, people can make permitted plan changes for
coverage beginning January 1, 2027. See Medicare's AEP guidance
(https://www.medicare.gov/health-drug-plans/open-enrollment). If you
leave Medicare Advantage for Original Medicare, consider whether you
need a standalone Part D plan and review your state-specific Medigap
rights; Medigap enrollment is not automatically guaranteed in every
situation.

MEDICARE ADVANTAGE OPEN ENROLLMENT PERIOD
=========================================

From January 1 through March 31, a person already enrolled in
Medicare Advantage can generally make one change: switch to another
Medicare Advantage plan, or return to Original Medicare and join a
standalone Part D plan. This is not a second AEP for people in
Original Medicare to switch standalone Part D plans.

PART D SPECIAL ENROLLMENT AFTER DRUG COVERAGE ENDS
==================================================

Part D timing is different from the Part B eight-month window.
Losing employer or union drug coverage generally creates a plan
enrollment window of two full months after the month coverage ends.
For an involuntary loss of creditable drug coverage, the window is
generally two full months after the loss or notice, whichever is
later. Other situations have different rules. Keep the notice
showing whether the prior drug coverage was creditable and confirm
your deadline using Medicare's Part D special-enrollment guidance
(https://www.medicare.gov/sign-up-change-plans/joining-a-health-or-drug-plan/special-circumstances-special-enrollment-periods).

FEDERAL MEDICARE COSTS FOR 2027
===============================

CMS announced the 2027 amounts on November 17, 2026. These are
federal amounts effective January 1, 2027. They are not plan quotes;
private plan premiums and cost sharing can differ. See the CMS 2027
Part A and B release
(https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/2027-medicare-parts-b-premiums-deductibles).

PART A
======

* Item: Inpatient hospital deductible (each benefit period)
  Amount: $1,776

* Item: Hospital coinsurance, days 61-90
  Amount: $444 per day

* Item: Lifetime reserve days
  Amount: $888 per day

* Item: Skilled nursing facility coinsurance, days 21-100
  Amount: $222 per day

* Item: Monthly premium with 30-39 Medicare-covered work quarters
  Amount: $328

* Item: Monthly premium with fewer than 30 quarters
  Amount: $597

Most people with at least 40 quarters of Medicare-covered work (or
an eligible spouse's record) pay no Part A premium. Eligibility
rules apply. The inpatient deductible is per benefit period, not a
once-per-year deductible.

PART B
======

* Item: Standard monthly premium
  Amount: $209.40

* Item: Annual deductible
  Amount: $292

People with higher income may pay an Income-Related Monthly
Adjustment Amount (IRMAA) in addition to the standard premium. The
premium and deductible do not describe every service's cost sharing.

PART D STANDARD BENEFIT
=======================

* Item: Maximum standard deductible
  Amount: $700

* Item: Annual out-of-pocket threshold for covered Part D drugs
  Amount: $2,400

The $700 figure is the maximum deductible for the defined standard
benefit; plans may use a lower deductible or a different allowed
benefit design. After qualifying out-of-pocket spending reaches the
2027 threshold-including certain amounts paid on your behalf under
Medicare's rules-the covered Part D drugs included under Medicare's
rules have no member cost sharing for the remainder of the calendar
year. Check Medicare's Part D cost guidance
(https://www.medicare.gov/health-drug-plans/part-d/basics/costs),
your plan's drug list, pharmacies, and other rules. The Part D
amount is separate from medical-service costs under Parts A and B.

INCOME-RELATED ADJUSTMENTS (IRMAA)
==================================

SSA generally uses tax information from two years earlier to
determine IRMAA, so 2027 determinations generally use 2025 income
information. SSA may use another available tax year in some
circumstances. If a qualifying life-changing event reduced your
income, ask SSA whether you can request a new determination. See
SSA's IRMAA income-year policy
(https://secure.ssa.gov/POMS.NSF/LNX/0601101010).

The following are 2027 monthly amounts from CMS. "Total Part B"
includes the $209.40 standard premium. Part D amount is the
additional IRMAA on top of the plan's own premium.

* Income: Individual income $112,000 or less; joint income $224,000 or less
  Part B total: $209.40
  Part D IRMAA: $0

* Income: Individual income over $112,000 through $141,000; joint over $224,000 through $282,000
  Part B total: $293.10
  Part D IRMAA: $15.40

* Income: Individual income over $141,000 through $177,000; joint over $282,000 through $354,000
  Part B total: $418.80
  Part D IRMAA: $39.70

* Income: Individual income over $177,000 through $212,000; joint over $354,000 through $424,000
  Part B total: $544.40
  Part D IRMAA: $64.00

* Income: Individual income over $212,000 but under $500,000; joint over $424,000 but under $750,000
  Part B total: $670.00
  Part D IRMAA: $88.30

* Income: Individual income $500,000 or more; joint income $750,000 or more
  Part B total: $711.80
  Part D IRMAA: $96.40

For married people filing separately who lived with a spouse during
the year, CMS uses separate thresholds:

* Income: $112,000 or less
  Part B total: $209.40
  Part D IRMAA: $0

* Income: Over $112,000 and under $388,000
  Part B total: $670.00
  Part D IRMAA: $88.30

* Income: $388,000 or more
  Part B total: $711.80
  Part D IRMAA: $96.40

Confirm your category and amount with the CMS/SSA notice; these
figures do not replace that determination.

LATE ENROLLMENT PENALTIES
=========================

* Part B: The premium generally increases 10% for each full 12-month
period you could have had Part B but did not, unless an exception
applies (such as a qualifying current-employment Special Enrollment
Period). The increase usually lasts as long as you have Part B.
* Part D: The penalty is generally 1% of the national base
beneficiary premium for each full month without Part D or other
creditable drug coverage after the eligible period. For 2027, CMS
set that base at $41.33 (CMS's 2027 base-premium release
(https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/medicare-part-d-2027-national-average-monthly-bid-amount-information);
published July 28, 2026); the penalty is rounded to the nearest
$0.10 and generally continues while you have Part D. Extra Help and
other exceptions can apply. This is not 1% of your individual plan
premium.
* Part A: If you must buy Part A and delay enrollment, the premium
may increase 10%; the higher premium generally lasts for twice the
number of years you delayed. Exceptions and eligibility details
apply.

These are general rules. Keep notices about prior coverage and
verify your personal situation with Medicare or Social Security
before delaying enrollment. See Medicare's penalty guidance
(https://www.medicare.gov/basics/costs/medicare-costs/avoid-penalties).

ORIGINAL MEDICARE, MEDICARE ADVANTAGE, AND MEDIGAP
==================================================

Original Medicare lets you use providers who accept Medicare,
subject to Medicare coverage and cost-sharing rules. Many people add
a standalone Part D plan and may consider Medigap to help with
certain Original Medicare cost sharing. Medigap does not work with
Medicare Advantage and does not generally include prescription drug
coverage.

Medigap's federal one-time open enrollment period generally lasts
six months beginning when you are 65 or older and enrolled in Part
B. After it ends, companies may be able to use medical underwriting
unless you have a guaranteed-issue right or your state provides
additional protections. State rules differ; see Medicare's Medigap
timing guide
(https://www.medicare.gov/health-drug-plans/medigap/ready-to-buy/when)
and contact your State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP)
for unbiased counseling.

Medicare Advantage plans have plan-specific networks, premiums,
prior authorization rules, and cost sharing. Check your doctors,
hospitals, medicines, pharmacy, and total expected costs in the
plan's current documents and Medicare Plan Finder. A plan's star
rating is a quality measure, not a guarantee that a provider, drug,
or benefit fits your needs.

IF YOU HAVE VA, TRICARE, OR CHAMPVA COVERAGE
============================================

These programs coordinate with Medicare differently. VA health care
is not a substitute for Medicare coverage outside the VA system;
non-VA care may require VA authorization. People eligible for
CHAMPVA generally need Medicare Parts A and B, with limited
exceptions. Eligible TRICARE For Life beneficiaries generally need
Parts A and B; Medicare usually pays first for services covered by
both in the United States. Confirm details with VA
(https://www.va.gov/resources/va-health-care-and-other-insurance/),
CHAMPVA
(https://www.va.gov/family-and-caregiver-benefits/health-and-disability/champva/),
or TRICARE (https://www.tricare.mil/medicare) before changing
coverage.

A PRACTICAL CHECKLIST
=====================

1. Write down your IEP dates and ask your benefits administrator how
current-employment coverage coordinates with Medicare.
2. Keep the employer or plan notice showing when medical and
creditable drug coverage ends.
3. Compare 2027 plan documents-not only premiums-including doctors,
prescriptions, pharmacies, deductibles, and out-of-pocket limits.
4. Confirm enrollment deadlines with Medicare or Social Security
before acting. Do not assume a Part B deadline also applies to Part
D.
5. Keep the official notices used for your enrollment and cost
decisions.
