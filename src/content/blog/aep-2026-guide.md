Every fall, Medicare beneficiaries get one guaranteed opportunity to review and change their coverage without a qualifying life event: the Annual Enrollment Period, or AEP. For AEP 2026, that window runs **October 15 through December 7, 2026**, with any changes taking effect **January 1, 2027** — what's often called Plan Year 2027 coverage.

If you're already enrolled in Medicare, understanding how to use this window well can mean the difference between a plan that quietly cost you more than it should have and one that actually fits your needs, doctors, and prescriptions for the year ahead.

## What AEP Actually Is

AEP is specifically for people **already enrolled** in Medicare who want to make a change to their existing coverage. It's different from the Initial Enrollment Period (the window around your 65th birthday when you first sign up) — AEP exists every single year, giving you a recurring checkpoint to reassess your coverage rather than locking you into a decision made years ago.

During AEP, you can:

- Switch from one Medicare Advantage plan to another
- Switch from one Part D prescription drug plan to another
- Switch from Original Medicare to a Medicare Advantage plan
- Switch from a Medicare Advantage plan back to Original Medicare
- Enroll in Part D for the first time, if you didn't previously have creditable drug coverage (though a late enrollment penalty may still apply if you had a coverage gap)

## What AEP Does Not Cover

AEP is not a catch-all enrollment window. A few important limits:

- **Medigap enrollment isn't tied to AEP.** If you switch from Medicare Advantage to Original Medicare during AEP and want to add a Medigap policy, that's a separate application, and outside your one-time Medigap Open Enrollment Period, insurers in most states can use medical underwriting to deny coverage or charge more.
- **It's not for first-time Medicare enrollment.** If you're not yet on Medicare at all, you're working with your Initial Enrollment Period, not AEP — see our [full enrollment guide](/enrollment) for that timeline.
- **Changes take effect January 1, not immediately.** Whatever you choose during AEP 2026 doesn't start until January 1, 2027 — you're still covered under your current plan through the end of the current plan year.

## How to Prepare Before AEP Opens

The weeks before October 15 are the best time to do your homework, not the weeks during AEP itself, when you're comparing plans under time pressure. A few things worth doing in advance:

1. **Read your plan's Annual Notice of Change (ANOC).** Your current Medicare Advantage or Part D plan sends this document every September, detailing what's changing for the next plan year — premiums, copays, and formulary updates. This is often the single most useful document for deciding whether to shop around.
2. **List your current medications and doctors.** Having this on hand makes comparing plans dramatically faster once AEP opens, since formulary and network status are the two details that matter most for most people.
3. **Decide what you're optimizing for.** Lower premium, broader provider network, better drug coverage, or extra benefits like dental and vision — knowing your priority before you start comparing plans keeps the process focused.

## Common AEP Mistakes

A few patterns come up again and again during AEP season:

- **Assuming last year's plan is still the best option** without checking the ANOC — formularies and networks change every year, even for plans you don't switch.
- **Comparing only the monthly premium**, without factoring in the plan's out-of-pocket maximum, copays, or how your specific prescriptions are covered.
- **Waiting until the last few days of AEP**, which leaves little time to research or get help if you decide you want a second opinion.
- **Not verifying provider networks directly** — a plan's marketing materials aren't always a reliable source for whether your specific doctor is currently in-network.
