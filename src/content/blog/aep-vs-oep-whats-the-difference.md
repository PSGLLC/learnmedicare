If you've seen the term "open enrollment" tied to two different Medicare windows this year, you're not imagining it. AEP 2026, the Annual Enrollment Period, is open right now through December 7. But once AEP closes, you'll start hearing about another window that opens January 1: the Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period, or OEP. Both involve the words "open enrollment," both let you change coverage, and both get mentioned constantly this time of year — but they are not the same program, and mixing them up can leave you without the option you thought you had. Here's what actually separates them, and which one applies to your situation.

## AEP: Open to Everyone, Any Number of Changes

AEP runs from October 15 through December 7 every year, and it's the one most people have heard of. During AEP 2026, anyone with Medicare can join, switch, or drop a Medicare Advantage or Part D plan for Plan Year 2027. You can move from Original Medicare into a Medicare Advantage plan, switch from one Medicare Advantage plan to another, change your standalone Part D drug plan, or drop Medicare Advantage and go back to Original Medicare. There's no limit on how many times you change your mind during this window — only your last submission before the December 7 deadline actually counts. That flexibility is one of the main things that makes AEP different from what comes next. Our full [AEP 2026 guide](/aep-guide/) covers the enrollment steps in detail if you haven't started your review yet.

## OEP: Limited to Medicare Advantage Members, One Change Only

OEP, the Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period, runs January 1 through March 31 each year. It is much narrower than AEP in two important ways. First, it's only available to people already enrolled in a Medicare Advantage plan — if you're in Original Medicare with or without a standalone Part D plan, OEP doesn't apply to you at all. Second, you only get one change. During OEP, someone in a Medicare Advantage plan can switch to a different Medicare Advantage plan, or drop Medicare Advantage entirely and return to Original Medicare (picking up a standalone Part D plan at the same time if needed). What you can't do during OEP is use it to join Medicare Advantage for the first time from Original Medicare, or switch standalone Part D plans without also leaving Medicare Advantage. Think of OEP as a narrow safety net for a decision made during AEP that isn't working out, not a second AEP.

## Don't Confuse OEP with the General Enrollment Period (GEP)

This is where things get genuinely confusing, because two completely different Medicare programs share the exact same calendar window: January 1 through March 31. The Medicare Advantage OEP described above is for people who already have Medicare Advantage. The General Enrollment Period (GEP) is a completely separate program for people who don't have Medicare yet because they missed their Initial Enrollment Period and don't qualify for a Special Enrollment Period. GEP lets someone sign up for Medicare Part A and/or Part B for the first time, but it often comes with late enrollment penalties attached. Our [enrollment periods page](/enrollment/) breaks down IEP, SEP, GEP, and AEP in one place, which is worth bookmarking since these terms get used interchangeably even though they serve very different purposes.

## Why This Distinction Matters Right Now

Because AEP 2026 is open until December 7, this is the moment to make your real decision for Plan Year 2027, not to put it off assuming OEP will give you a full do-over in January. If you wait and miss AEP's December 7 deadline, your options narrow sharply. Someone currently in Medicare Advantage would have OEP as a fallback, but only for one change, and only between Medicare Advantage options or back to Original Medicare — not for switching a standalone Part D plan on its own. Someone in Original Medicare who misses AEP has no equivalent fallback window at all until the Annual Enrollment Period rolls around again next fall, unless a qualifying life event triggers a Special Enrollment Period. ## A Quick Way to Keep Them Straight

A simple way to remember the difference: AEP is the big, flexible window open to everyone with Medicare, running October 15 through December 7, with unlimited changes allowed. OEP is the narrow, one-time fix available only to current Medicare Advantage members, running January 1 through March 31. If a term or acronym here still isn't clicking, the [Medicare glossary](/glossary/) has plain-English definitions for AEP, OEP, GEP, and the other enrollment period abbreviations that tend to blur together.

Knowing which window actually applies to you can save a lot of stress later in the year. If you're in Medicare Advantage and AEP ends up not working out the way you hoped, OEP is there as a limited backstop — but it's not a substitute for taking AEP seriously while it's still open. The safest approach is to treat December 7 as your real deadline and use the weeks before it to confirm your Plan Year 2027 coverage actually fits your doctors, medications, and budget.
