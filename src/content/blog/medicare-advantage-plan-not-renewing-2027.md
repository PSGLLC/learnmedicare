Every fall, a number of Medicare Advantage members open their mail to find a notice they weren't expecting: their plan is leaving their county, shrinking its service area, or exiting the market altogether for the coming year. If that's you this AEP 2026 season, the first thing to know is that this isn't something you caused, and it isn't the dead end it might feel like. You have real deadlines, real options, and in many cases, extra protections that members who are simply switching plans voluntarily don't get.

This guide walks through what a non-renewal notice actually means, the decisions in front of you, and the steps to take before your coverage changes on January 1, 2027.

## What a Non-Renewal Notice Actually Means

Medicare requires insurance companies to notify affected members in writing if a Medicare Advantage plan won't be offered in their area for the next plan year. This is different from a routine Annual Notice of Change, which describes how your same plan is changing costs or benefits — a non-renewal notice tells you the plan itself won't exist where you live starting January 1, 2027.

Carriers make this decision for business reasons: low enrollment in a county, network contract issues, or a broader decision to pull out of certain markets. It has nothing to do with your health, your claims history, or anything you did as a member. If you're unsure whether the letter you received is a non-renewal notice or a standard Annual Notice of Change, our [guide to reading your ANOC](/blog/how-to-read-your-anoc/) explains what each document covers and how to tell them apart.

## Your Deadlines and Options Right Now

Because AEP 2026 is open (through December 7, 2026), you already have a built-in window to act. Here's what's available to you:

- **Switch to a different Medicare Advantage plan.** Other carriers may still offer Medicare Advantage coverage in your county. Our [guide to comparing Medicare Advantage plans](/blog/compare-medicare-advantage-plans-aep/) walks through how to check that your doctors and prescriptions are covered before you choose.
- **Move to Original Medicare and add a standalone Part D plan.** This keeps you on traditional Medicare, with the flexibility to see any provider who accepts Medicare, plus separate drug coverage.
- **Move to Original Medicare and apply for a Medigap policy.** This is where non-renewal members often get a protection that voluntary switchers don't: guaranteed issue rights, covered in detail below.

If your non-renewal notice arrived close to the AEP deadline, or you need more time than December 7 allows, you likely qualify for a dedicated Special Enrollment Period tied to the plan's exit. That SEP gives you additional months beyond the normal AEP window to enroll in a new Medicare Advantage or Part D plan, so a tight calendar shouldn't force a rushed decision. Our [enrollment periods guide](/enrollment) explains how Special Enrollment Periods work alongside AEP, the GEP, and other windows.

## Why Guaranteed Issue Rights Matter Here

Outside of your one-time Medigap Open Enrollment Period around turning 65, insurance companies are generally allowed to use medical underwriting when you apply for a Medigap policy — meaning they can ask health questions, charge you more, or turn you down. But federal law carves out specific situations, called guaranteed issue rights, where an insurer has to sell you a Medigap policy regardless of your health history.

Having your Medicare Advantage plan leave the market or your service area is one of those guaranteed issue situations. If this applies to you, an insurer offering Medigap in your area generally can't deny you a policy or charge you a higher premium because of a pre-existing condition. This is a meaningful difference from a member who voluntarily drops Medicare Advantage to try Original Medicare outside one of these protected situations — that person could face full underwriting.

Guaranteed issue rights come with their own timeline, usually a window of 63 days from when your old coverage ends, so this isn't something to sit on. If you think Medigap might be the right fit, it's worth confirming your guaranteed issue eligibility and applying within that window rather than waiting until your new plan year is already underway. Our [Medicare Advantage vs. Medigap comparison](/blog/medicare-advantage-vs-medigap/) can help you weigh which direction makes sense for your situation.

## Checking What Changes for Your Doctors and Prescriptions

Whether you move to a new Medicare Advantage plan or over to Original Medicare, the practical question is the same: will your doctors, hospital, and pharmacy still be covered, and at what cost? A plan leaving the market doesn't mean your providers are disappearing — it means your insurance arrangement with them is.

Before enrolling anywhere new, pull together:

- A current list of your prescriptions and dosages, to check against a new plan's formulary.
- The names of your primary doctors and any specialists you see regularly, to confirm they're in-network for a Medicare Advantage option, or simply note that Original Medicare works with any provider who accepts Medicare.
- Any upcoming procedures or ongoing treatment that might be affected by a coverage gap or network change.

A non-renewal notice is unwelcome news, but it isn't a gap in your coverage if you act within the windows available to you. Between the remainder of AEP 2026, a likely Special Enrollment Period, and guaranteed issue protections for Medigap, you have more room to make a good decision than the letter itself might suggest.
