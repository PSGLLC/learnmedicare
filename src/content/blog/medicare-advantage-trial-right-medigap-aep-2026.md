AEP 2026 is open through December 7, and most of the advice this time of year is about comparing Medicare Advantage or Part D plans for Plan Year 2027. But there's a smaller, less talked-about option that matters a lot if it applies to you: the Medicare Advantage trial right. If you joined a Medicare Advantage plan within the last 12 months, this federal protection may let you drop it and buy a Medigap policy without an insurance company asking a single question about your health. Outside of this right, that same move usually means medical underwriting, which can mean a higher premium, an exclusion, or an outright denial. Here's how the trial right actually works, who qualifies, and how it fits together with what's happening during AEP right now.

## What the Trial Right Actually Protects You From

Normally, Medigap policies are sold with medical underwriting attached. The one guaranteed window everyone gets is the 6-month Medigap Open Enrollment Period that starts the month you're both 65 or older and enrolled in Part B — our [Medigap guide](/medigap-guide/) covers that window in detail. Miss it, and in most states an insurer can ask about your health history, charge you more, add exclusions for pre-existing conditions, or decline to sell you a policy at all.

The trial right is one of a handful of federal guaranteed issue rights that get you back to that same protected status — no health questions, no higher premium for being sick, no denial — even though your Medigap Open Enrollment Period has already passed. It exists specifically because federal law wants people to be able to try Medicare Advantage without permanently losing their shot at Medigap if it doesn't work out.

## Who Actually Qualifies

The trial right covers two specific situations, both tied to the first 12 months after you join Medicare Advantage for the first time:

1. **You joined Medicare Advantage when you first became eligible for Medicare**, usually at 65, instead of choosing Original Medicare with a Medigap policy. If you decide within your first 12 months that you want Original Medicare and Medigap after all, you have guaranteed issue rights to buy a policy.
2. **You had a Medigap policy already and dropped it to try Medicare Advantage for the first time.** If you switch back to Original Medicare within 12 months of that first-time Medicare Advantage enrollment, you generally have the right to get your old Medigap policy back from the same insurer, or another policy with guaranteed issue rights if that exact plan is no longer sold.

Both situations require this to be your first time in a Medicare Advantage plan. If you've already used Medicare Advantage for a few years, switched back once before, or you're past that initial 12-month window, the trial right doesn't apply again — any future move from Medicare Advantage to Medigap would typically go through medical underwriting instead, unless a different guaranteed issue right applies, such as your current plan [leaving the market entirely](/blog/medicare-advantage-plan-not-renewing-2027/).

## How This Connects to What's Happening During AEP Right Now

AEP and the trial right are two completely separate mechanisms that happen to overlap for some people this time of year. AEP, running through December 7, is the yearly window where you can drop Medicare Advantage and return to Original Medicare for Plan Year 2027 — see our [AEP vs. OEP comparison](/blog/aep-vs-oep-whats-the-difference/) if you're unclear on how AEP relates to other enrollment windows. The trial right doesn't run on that same calendar. It's tied to your personal enrollment date in Medicare Advantage, not to AEP itself.

That means if you joined a Medicare Advantage plan for Plan Year 2026 sometime between January and this fall, you may still be inside your 12-month trial right window right now, during AEP 2026. You can use AEP to make the actual switch back to Original Medicare, effective January 1, 2027, and then separately apply for a Medigap policy using your trial right — generally within 63 days of when your Medicare Advantage coverage ends. Missing that 63-day application window is the biggest risk here, since the trial right itself doesn't wait for you.

## Why This Matters More Than It Might Seem

A lot of people assume that once they've picked Medicare Advantage, going back to Medigap later means starting from scratch with full underwriting, and that assumption sometimes talks people out of trying Medicare Advantage in the first place, or keeps them in a plan that genuinely isn't working. The trial right exists precisely to remove that risk for a limited time. For a full side-by-side of how these two paths differ day to day, our [Medicare Advantage vs. Medigap comparison](/blog/medicare-advantage-vs-medigap/) walks through costs, networks, and flexibility, and our broader [coverage comparison guide](/coverage/) covers how all of this fits with the rest of your Medicare choices.

If you're not sure whether you're still inside your 12-month window, check the exact date your current Medicare Advantage plan started — not the date you decided to get Medicare, but the date that specific plan's coverage began. That start date is what the 12 months counts from. If any of the terms here, like guaranteed issue or medical underwriting, aren't fully clear, the [Medicare glossary](/glossary/) has plain-English definitions worth bookmarking before you make a call either way.

## What to Do If You Think You Qualify

Start by confirming your Medicare Advantage start date and counting forward 12 months from it. If you're still inside that window, decide whether you want to use AEP to switch back to Original Medicare now, for a January 1, 2027 effective date, or whether a qualifying event might open a different Special Enrollment Period sooner. Once your Medicare Advantage coverage actually ends, apply for your Medigap policy promptly — don't let the 63-day application window run out while you're still comparing options, since the trial right protection itself doesn't extend past that deadline. Keeping documentation of your original Medicare Advantage enrollment date on hand will also make the Medigap application itself move faster, since the insurer will want to confirm your trial right eligibility before issuing the policy.
